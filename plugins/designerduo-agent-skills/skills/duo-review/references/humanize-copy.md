Style rules for any copy this skill (or cro-review) suggests, rewrites, or generates. Apply these to every headline, CTA, or paragraph rewrite offered during a review, not just when asked to "write copy."

## Hard rule: no em-dashes
Never use em-dashes anywhere in suggested copy. Use a period, a comma, a colon, "and"/"but", or split into two sentences instead. This is a firm user preference, not a style suggestion to weigh.

## Avoid the tells that make copy read as AI-generated
- "Not just X, but Y" used more than once on a page.
- Rule-of-three adjective stacking ("fast, easy, and reliable") repeated across multiple headlines.
- Throat-clearing openers ("In today's fast-paced world...", "In an ever-changing landscape...").
- Buzzword filler with no concrete backing: leverage, seamless, robust, unlock, elevate, game-changer, cutting-edge, revolutionize, holistic, best-in-class, world-class, synergy, empower.
- "It's not about X, it's about Y" as a rhetorical crutch.
- Semicolons in customer-facing copy. They read as written, not spoken.
- Excessive hedging or qualifier stacking that dilutes a claim instead of committing to it.

## Write toward specificity, not abstraction
Concrete nouns and real numbers beat vague superlatives (this is the same specificity principle in `psychology.md`). "3,400+ leaders trained" beats "trusted by many." A rewritten headline should name a real outcome, not describe a feeling about the product.

## Sound like a person talking to one other person
- Contractions are good. Sentence fragments for emphasis are fine. Starting a sentence with "And" or "But" is fine.
- Vary sentence length on purpose. A short sentence after a longer one lands harder than three medium sentences in a row.
- Read any suggested line out loud (mentally). If it doesn't sound like something an actual person would say to a friend, rewrite it plainer.
- Write to "you," the one visitor reading it, not to "our valued customers" or "everyone."

## Check before handing off a rewrite
Every copy suggestion in a review should pass: no em-dash, no buzzword filler, states something specific and real, and sounds like a person wrote it. If a suggested rewrite fails any of these, redo it before including it in the report.
